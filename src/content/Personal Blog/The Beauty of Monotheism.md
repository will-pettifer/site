Following on from what we tried to establish in *The Cosmological Argument*, the question of how God communicates with us suddenly becomes very important. It seems natural then to begin by examining religion, given that most religions claim to have this line of communication, and that the vast majority of human beings are religious.

However, what we established actually refutes a lot of religious arguments. Rather than focus on metaphysical and logical problems with major religions, e.g. *the logical problem of the Trinity*, we will instead try to draw out and understand what may be the most beautiful idea humanity has ever been blessed with.

### A Personal God
There is a view of God that is prevalent among many faiths, that of a god who is intertwined with the Universe. From Hindus, to Sikhs, to Gnostics, to Radical Christians, so many religions treat 'god' as a force of nature. This is a god that permeates its creation, with each and every person being themselves part of the divine. God enters the creation, taking on flesh and incarnating as a man. Take, for example, the Avatars of the Hindu gods, or the Son in the Christian Trinity.

>"O Arjun, I am seated in the heart of all living entities. I am the beginning, middle, and end of all beings."
>
>*(Bhagavad Gita 10:20)*

>"The One God is diffused in the ten directions. Behold God in all the earth and sky. In the water, on the land, in the forests and mountains, and in the nether regions of the underworld, the Merciful Transcendent Lord is abiding. The Lord God is in all mind and matter, subtle and manifest. O Nanak, the Gurmukh realizes God."
>
>*(Guru Granth Sahib, Page 319)*

>"Jesus said, 'I am the All. The All came forth from me, and the All attained to me. Split a piece of wood; I am there. Lift up the stone, and you will find me there.'"
>
>*(Gospel of Thomas, Saying 77)*

>"God is not 'out there'. He is in Bonhoeffer's words 'the "beyond" in the midst of our life', a depth of reality reached 'not on the borders of life but at its centre', 2  not by any flight of the alone to the alone, but, in Kierkegaard's fine phrase, by 'a deeper immersion in existence'. For the word 'God' denotes the ultimate depth of all our being, the creative ground and meaning of all our existence.
>
>So conditioned for us is the word 'God' by associations with a Being out there that Tillich warns us that to make the necessary transposition, 'you must forget everything traditional that you have learned about God, perhaps even that word itself'. Indeed, the line between those who believe in God and those who do not bears little relation to their profession of the existence or non-existence of such a Being."
>
>*(J. Robinson, Honest to God, p.28)*

To many, this kind of view can seem very appealing. It sounds beautiful and spiritual, it claims a level of intimacy with God, it makes humans near equals to God, and it has a high overlap with esotericism which lends it apparent legitimacy without requiring any rigour in its intellectual claims. However, on closer inspection, this view does nothing but negate God's majesty, and sever the relationship between God and humanity.

You can relate to inanimate objects; there is a wonderful experience of connecting with nature, or eating a delicious meal. However, there is no comparison with the depth and richness of a human relationship.
### An Altruistic God
God has no need of anything. He is perfectly satisfied. Whatever desire He might have, He could satisfy with no conditions or barriers. Everything that occurs occurs because it is what He willed, so no one can ever challenge Him. He is capable of all things, and so He always gets His way. He has no need of His creation, and if it were to displease Him, He could destroy it as easily as He created it.

This can make God seem cold, and uncompromising. His lack of need makes Him distant to His creation, His sovereignty makes Him a tyrant. The truth is exactly the opposite. The fact that God has no need of us, yet still created us, that He can't be benefited by us, yet still communicates with us, that He could destroy anyone who rebels, yet still gave us free will, this makes God perfectly selfless. Everything that God does is Altruistic. This total lack of external need means that God literally cannot be selfish.

It takes humility to understand this point. We have to be able to see ourselves for what we really are. We have to understand just how small we are to get why this matters and why this is so beautiful.

Consider the compassion you feel towards an ant. You could easily step on this creature without even realising. Now compare the difference between the comparison of you to an ant, and the comparison of God to you. If you feel no affection towards an ant, then how should God, the creator of everything, feel about you? And yet, while you step on an ant without noticing, God has given you a mind, and a 1st person experience. He's given you every breath you ever had, a free will, a body, He's fed you your whole life, and He has given you the mental faculties to get to know him. This is a level of compassion that humanity will never reach, nor will they ever deserve it for themselves.
